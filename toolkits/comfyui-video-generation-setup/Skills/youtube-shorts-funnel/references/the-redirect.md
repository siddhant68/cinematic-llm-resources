# The Redirect

Everything about converting a Shorts viewer into a long-form watcher. This is
the part of Shorts strategy that most guidance treats as an afterthought and
that matters most when the goal is long-form watch hours.

## The arithmetic

Worth doing explicitly, because it sets expectations and reveals which lever
actually moves the target.

Working from a 4,000 long-form watch hour goal:

```
4,000 hours                          = 240,000 watch minutes
long-form runtime 40 min
average view duration 30%            = ~12 minutes per view
240,000 / 12                         = ~20,000 long-form views needed
```

Then the conversion step, where published figures vary a lot:

| Assumed Short→long conversion | Shorts views required |
|---|---|
| 4.5% (optimistic, blended marketing data) | ~445,000 |
| 2% (reasonable planning number) | ~1,000,000 |
| 1% (pessimistic; some sources put related-video linking below this) | ~2,000,000 |

The spread is wide because published numbers blend niches with wildly different
viewer intent. Plan with 2%, hope for better in a high-intent technical niche.

**The important thing this reveals: Shorts volume is not the strongest lever.**
Average view duration is. Doubling AVD from 12 to 24 minutes halves the required
views, from 20,000 to 10,000, which halves the required Shorts views too. Making
the long-form more watchable is cheaper than doubling Shorts output, and it
compounds with every other traffic source.

The second thing it reveals: long-form needs its own discovery. Browse, search
and suggested traffic do the bulk of the work for most channels reaching this
threshold. Shorts accelerate; they rarely carry it alone.

Use this to set the real strategy: long-form retention first, long-form
discoverability second, Shorts as amplification third. A Shorts-only plan
against a long-form watch-hours goal is fighting the platform's own structure.

## Why the click stopped happening by accident

The Shorts feed and long-form recommendations run as separate systems. A Short
performing well does not push the channel's long-form videos into
recommendations, and audience overlap between the two is small — often cited
around 10%, meaning Shorts and long-form largely reach different people.

The practical read: the bridge is not a bonus on top of a good Short. It is the
entire mechanism. If it is not built deliberately into the script, it does not
exist.

## The four bridge surfaces

| Surface | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **In-video callout** | Spoken line plus on-screen text, in the last few seconds | Strongest single surface. Must name the destination specifically. |
| **Pinned comment** | First comment, pinned, carrying the link | Catches viewers who finished and dropped into comments, which is a high-intent moment. |
| **End frame** | The final 1-2 seconds, with a visible label | Cheap. Pairs with or competes with the loop; see below. |
| **Channel surface** | Shorts tab consistency and channel trailer | When a Short lands, viewers tap the channel name. If the tab is topically coherent and the trailer is the right long-form, they convert. If the tab is a grab-bag, they leave. |

Bridges land far more reliably when three or more fire together on the same
Short. Writing only the spoken line and skipping the pinned comment gives up
most of the effect for no reason.

## Writing the callout

Three jobs in one or two sentences:

1. **Reference what they just watched.** Continuity, not a hard pivot.
2. **Name the destination concretely.** By topic, and by title where it fits.
3. **Name the withheld thing.** What is in the long-form that is not here.

That third element is the lever. A callout promising more of the same earns
almost nothing, because the viewer just got the thing and has no reason to spend
forty minutes on a longer version of it. A callout naming something specific and
absent creates a reason.

Weak: "Full video on my channel if you want to learn more."

Strong: "This is one of six failure modes. The other five, and the fix for each,
are in the character generation breakdown on my channel."

Strong: "I show the system catching this live, including the one it got wrong,
in the full episode."

The pattern: **name the gap, not the length.** "Longer" is not a benefit.
"The four I did not cover here" is.

Place it in the last five to eight seconds, with on-screen text reinforcing the
spoken line, since much of the audience is watching muted.

## Writing the pinned comment

Short, specific, link included. The structure that works:

> The full [topic] breakdown is here, including [one specific thing not in this
> Short]: [link]

Same lever as the callout. "Same content but longer" earns nothing. "The live
run where it fails and repairs itself, with timestamps" earns the click.

Pin it immediately on upload. An unpinned comment is buried within minutes.

## Loop versus click

The genuine tension in Shorts strategy, and one most guidance glosses over.

**The case for loops.** Shorts auto-replay, so a clean loop pushes percentage
viewed past 100%, and re-watches are weighted as a distribution signal. Loops
are close to free retention, and retention buys reach.

**The problem.** A viewer held in a loop is a viewer not leaving to watch the
long-form. The techniques are in direct competition for the same final seconds:
a loop pulls attention back to the start, a bridge pushes it out to another
video. The strongest loop design — ending on a tease whose payoff sits at the
start of the replay — is the most hostile to the click, because it deliberately
traps the viewer inside the Short.

**How to choose.** Ask what this Short is for:

| Situation | Choose |
|---|---|
| A long-form exists and the goal is watch hours | **Click.** End on the bridge. Accept lower percentage-viewed. |
| No suitable long-form exists yet | **Loop.** Maximize reach and subscribers; add a bridge later by pinning a comment when the long-form ships. |
| The Short is early in a batch and still finding its audience | **Loop** to establish distribution, then post a variant optimized for the click once you know the topic lands. |
| The Short is already converting well | **Click**, harder. Do not sacrifice a working bridge for a marginal retention gain. |

A middle path exists and is often right: a soft visual loop — the last frame
matching the first — while the *audio and text* carry the bridge. This gets some
replay weight without the final beat actively pulling attention back inward.
What it must not do is end on an unresolved tease, which is a loop technique
wearing a bridge costume.

Whichever you choose, state it in the script. An undeclared choice usually
becomes a weak version of both.

## Why not "subscribe"

Three reasons, for this goal specifically:

1. **A subscriber is not a watch hour.** Subscribers acquired through Shorts
   convert to long-form viewing at low rates. The subscription itself moves the
   1,000-subs half of the requirement but does nothing for the 4,000 hours.
2. **The ask is redundant.** The subscribe control sits in the Shorts player
   where people are already swiping, and taps happen with or without a prompt.
   Asking spends attention on something that was going to happen anyway.
3. **It replaces a better ask.** The callout is the one moment of directed
   attention the Short has. Spending it on a subscribe means not spending it on
   a click.

Subscribe asks make sense when building toward the subscriber threshold with no
long-form library to point at. Once the library exists, the click is worth more.

## Measuring it

In Studio, traffic-source attribution on the long-form video shows arrivals from
Shorts. Track this per Short rather than in aggregate, because the distribution
is heavily skewed: a small number of Shorts typically send most of the traffic.

The diagnostic split that matters:

- **Low views** → retention problem. Fix the first three seconds.
- **Good views, no clicks** → funnel problem. Fix the bridge. The Short is
  working and giving away its only valuable output.

The second failure is the common one and the easy one to miss, because the
vanity metrics look healthy. A Short with strong views and no clicks is not a
success with a minor flaw; against a watch-hours goal it is a failure that looks
like a success.
