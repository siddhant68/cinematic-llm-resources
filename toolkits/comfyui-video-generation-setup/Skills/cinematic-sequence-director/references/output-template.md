# Output Template

Use this as the default shape. Adapt section names to the activity; do not mechanically include irrelevant sections.

## A. Directorial map

Keep this concise.

- **Total duration:** [target]
- **Generation plan:** [Clip 1 duration] + [Clip 2 duration] [+ optional Clip 3]
- **Continuity authority:** [approved prior render / supplied start frame / script / references]
- **Performance geography:** [fixed landmark map and zone assignments]
- **Lighting geography:** [fixed source direction + progression]
- **Continuity carryover:** [hair / footwear / props / body condition / exact last-known placements]
- **Continuity bridge:** [exact end of one clip -> exact opening of next]
- **Energy arc:** [beginning -> escalation -> resolution]
- **Risk allocation:** [where performer complexity is high and camera is intentionally simpler; where camera may become more expressive]

Do not add the optional lines when they are irrelevant.

## B. Clip prompt

# [Activity / Clip N — Cinematic title]

[Opening paragraph: duration, tone, strict reference identity/costume/hair/footwear, environment, continuity authority, and performance intent. Make the clip understandable by itself.]

---

## MASTER GEOGRAPHY — NEVER CHANGE THIS

[Fixed overhead map, landmarks, light source. Pair important numerical anchors with plain-language landmarks.]

State clearly when useful:

**THE CAMERA MAY MOVE. THE PERFORMER MAY TURN OR TRAVEL AS DIRECTED. THE SET ITSELF MUST NEVER ROTATE, MIRROR OR REARRANGE.**

---

## CONTINUITY LEDGER

[Include only when persistent objects or state changes matter. Record last-known prop locations, holder states, hair/footwear condition, exertion, and which changes must occur on screen.]

Examples:

- `Ribbon: same single ribbon, lying on north bench where it was left; pickup must originate there.`
- `Slippers: aligned directly beneath north bench; remain off her feet until the departure transition.`
- `Hair: fully loose at opening -> upper half tied only after the visible ribbon action.`

---

## PERFORMANCE ZONE / ROUTE

[Exact zone or route, approximate distance from landmark, allowed travel area.]

Explain what camera changes do NOT imply about the performer's world position.

---

## OPENING STATE AND ORIENTATION

[Exact physical starting mark, facing direction, body state, previous-scene continuity. If a supplied start anchor or approved previous render is authoritative, say so.]

---

## OPENING CAMERA AND LIGHT

[Wide/master/medium/close, lens feel, camera location, movement, world-space light direction, key/fill/rim/background behavior.]

---

## [MOVEMENT PHRASE 1 — USE NAME WHEN HELPFUL]

[Literal choreography and performance.]

[Camera response, lens/scale, direction, what the viewer should notice.]

[Lighting/physics only when they materially change.]

---

## [MOTIVATED DETAIL / CLOSE-UP]

[Exact detail: foot, hand, face, eyes, breath, costume, prop, etc.]

Use only when it answers a story or performance question. Prefer stable, landing, recovery, repeated, or lower-risk moments for difficult close coverage.

Do not omit a motivated close-up merely because another successful render happened not to use one; absence is not evidence of failure.

State that this is still the same world-space position when ambiguity is possible.

---

## [MOVEMENT PHRASE 2]

[Continue the motion logically. Preserve footprint/route and prop state.]

---

## [OPTIONAL ENERGY PEAK]

[Use more dynamic performer action or camera only if the other complexity axis remains readable. When performer action is difficult, simplify camera.]

---

## ENDING / CONTINUITY TRANSITION

[Exact action, camera, final body/location state, prop state, hair/footwear state, and exertion state.]

For a multi-clip sequence, make the edit bridge explicit:

`END CLIP 1 when ...`

or

`Clip 2 begins with the same action from ...`

If using an occlusion, partial/substantial natural obscuration may be enough; do not demand perfect hiding unless required.

---

## CAMERA RHYTHM

Summarize the visual grammar in one line, for example:

**wide geography -> sustained traveling full-body -> stable hero action -> expressive recovery close-up -> moving medium -> architectural release**

Then add 3–6 activity-specific camera cautions.

Treat this as a prioritized rhythm rather than a requirement for a separate hard cut at every arrow when the duration is tight.

---

## PERFORMANCE ARC

[Short progression of emotional/physical state. Express transformations through behavior: breath, weight, shoulders, eyes, posture, expression.]

---

## HARD CONTINUITY CONSTRAINTS

Only include constraints relevant to this sequence, such as:

- same character identity;
- same costume and hair state;
- same footwear/jewelry/props;
- prop pickup from the exact last-visible location;
- same environment geometry;
- fixed world-space light direction;
- explicit zone/route;
- no teleporting between camera changes;
- realistic foot plants, hands, fabric, hair and jewelry;
- no inappropriate modern objects or movement vocabulary;
- no unnecessary background performers;
- no direct look to camera unless requested.

Finish with one sentence describing the intended cinematic impression.

## C. Style expectations

- Write as a director giving production-ready instructions, not as a screenplay critic.
- Be visually specific and physically coherent.
- Prefer flowing prose plus strong section headings over terse metadata dumps.
- Use uppercase emphasis sparingly for continuity-critical facts.
- Give enough technical camera detail to shape the result without turning the prompt into a robotic frame list.
- Preserve the user's desired tone: calm, energetic, playful, tense, romantic, intimate, etc.
- Preserve validated directing principles across scenes, but adapt surface choreography and camera tempo to the new activity.
- Do not generalize from an untested omission. Distinguish validated success, observed failure, and untested hypotheses.
