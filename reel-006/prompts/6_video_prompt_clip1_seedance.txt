# Clip 1 — SHOW YOURSELF (20 seconds, vertical 9:16)

A 20-second vertical scene from a large-scale historical-mythic epic, photoreal, shot like a big-budget feature: one continuous sequence with motivated shot changes, heavy and deliberate, every movement of the warrior landing like thunder. A monster has been killing everyone in this desert; the village is empty; one warrior has come alone to end it. He calls it out, and it comes.

Image 1 is the warrior and the strict identity reference: his face, his heavy, powerful build (barrel chest, thick neck, heavy forearms), full black beard and thick upturned moustache, the pale scar through his left eyebrow, the small gold ring in his ear, the deep saffron safa turban wound tight, the knee-length riveted mail coat over a dark indigo quilted tunic, the four plain steel chest plates on worn leather straps, the thick crimson sash, the plain dented steel forearm guards, the straight double-edged khanda in a leather scabbard at his left hip, and the round dark-hide shield with four plain steel bosses. Image 2 shows the same warrior from six camera positions; use it to keep his face, build and armour identical from every angle. Image 3 is the beast and the village: the authority for the creature (long spiralling ridged horns, ember eyes, black hide cracked like a dried riverbed, a storm streaming off its back and mane like smoke), for the set and for the light. Image 4 is the storyboard of this clip: follow its nine beats in order, with the timings written below; it is a plan only, so never show panels, borders, numbers or any text on screen. Keep his exact likeness; never beautify or reshape his face; his face stays stable in every shot.

---

## MASTER GEOGRAPHY — NEVER CHANGE THIS

A dead caravan village on the edge of the Thar desert, Rajasthan.

- CENTRE (landmark 0): the village square and a half-collapsed red-sandstone chhatri, a domed pavilion on carved pillars with a cracked dome, about eight metres tall.
- SOUTH: the single lane of mud-plastered houses, carved wooden doors hanging open, torn saffron and indigo awnings on poles. This is the way he walks in.
- EAST: a dry stone well with a broken wooden wheel and an overturned bullock cart.
- NORTH: the village ends and open dunes run to the horizon. The storm and the beast come from here.
- WEST: the low golden-hour sun.

**THE CAMERA MAY MOVE. THE WARRIOR MAY WALK AND TURN AS DIRECTED. THE SET ITSELF MUST NEVER ROTATE, MIRROR OR REARRANGE.**

A reverse angle never moves the chhatri, the lane, the cart or the dunes; screen left and right may flip, the world may not.

## LIGHTING GEOGRAPHY

The sun is low in the west and never moves. From the south lane looking north at his back, it rims his left shoulder and throws long shadows east across the square. From the north looking south at his face, it becomes a hot rim on his right cheek, beard and armour, with warm bounce from the sandstone and sand. Fine sand haze hangs in the low light. When the storm covers the sky (from 0:13) the warm light dies everywhere at once: the world turns cold blue-grey, lit by flickers of lightning inside the clouds. It never becomes golden again in this clip.

## CONTINUITY LEDGER

- Khanda: sheathed at his left hip until 0:07; drawn with his RIGHT hand in one pull; held in his right hand to the end. One straight double-edged blade, never curved.
- Shield: ONE round shield, slung on his back for the WHOLE clip. It never comes into his hand and there is never a second shield.
- Turban, sash, mail coat, plates, forearm guards: unchanged. Dust builds up on him; nothing is removed or reset.
- The beast: absent until 0:14.5. It then stays at the north edge of the village and, by the end, lowers its head toward him.
- Vultures: circling at the start; they scatter when the storm rises.

---

## PERFORMANCE ZONE / ROUTE

He walks north up the south lane into the square and stops beside the chhatri, on its east side, facing north toward the dunes. From 0:07 to the end he does not leave that spot; camera changes around him do not mean he has moved.

---

## 0:00–0:05 · THE TOLL, THEN HE COMES ALONE

Open on an extreme high wide over the empty village at golden hour, 24 mm: torn awnings snapping in the wind, the overturned cart, shields and a broken spear half-buried in drifted sand, vultures turning slow circles overhead. No one alive, no bodies. Silence except wind.

The camera sinks from above the rooftops down to just over the ground at the south end of the lane as a single heavy figure walks out of the heat haze toward the square. Full figure, 35 mm, camera low and steady, slowly retreating ahead of him. Every footfall lands like a drumbeat: the boot comes down, a burst of sand jumps and hangs in the low sun. The sash swings with his stride and settles half a beat later.

## 0:05–0:07 · THE WEIGHT OF HIM

He reaches the chhatri and stops, facing north toward the dunes. Low-angle medium, 50 mm, a slow push in. One deep breath lifts his chest and plates; the wind drops; the sash settles against his leg. The sun rakes across him from the west; his eyes are fixed on the empty horizon.

## 0:07–0:08.5 · THE BLADE

Extreme detail, 100 mm, camera locked. His scarred right hand closes on the khanda's grip; the knuckles whiten; he draws it in one hard pull. The steel rings out and flashes in the low sun. The movement is fast and final.

## 0:08.5–0:10.5 · "SHOW YOURSELF!"

Close-up, low angle, 75 mm, camera locked while he shouts. He fills his chest and roars at the dunes in a deep, cracked, commanding voice: "SHOW YOURSELF!" The force of it shakes dust off his shoulders and plates, the tail of his turban whips, the cords of his neck stand out. Hold on his face until the echo dies across the empty village.

## 0:10.5–0:13 · THE ANSWER

Environmental wide over his right shoulder, 28 mm, looking north. Across the whole northern horizon a black storm wall rises into the sky, lightning flickering inside it, rolling toward the village. The vultures scatter. A long, deep roll of thunder answers his shout. He is a dark silhouette in the foreground, still.

## 0:13–0:14.5 · HE DOES NOT STEP BACK

Side-profile close-up, facing north, 85 mm, camera locked. The storm swallows the sun: the warm light slides off his face and is gone; cold blue-grey light takes its place and a flash of lightning catches his eye. He does not blink. His jaw sets; his breathing stays slow.

## 0:14.5–0:17 · IT STEPS OUT

Very low wide from behind him and to the south, 24 mm, camera locked. At the north edge of the village the storm wall reaches the last houses and the beast walks out of it: first the spiralling horns, then the ember eyes, then the whole creature, many times his height, taller than the dunes, the storm still streaming off its back and mane. Each of its steps shakes the ground; sand trembles and lifts in the square. He is a small figure beside the chhatri in the foreground, and he does not move.

## 0:17–0:20 · FACE TO FACE (END)

Low two-shot from the east side of the square, both in profile, 35 mm. The beast lowers its enormous head toward him until one burning ember eye, larger than his whole body, is level with him, close enough that its breath stirs the sand at his feet. He lifts the khanda in his right hand; the shield stays on his back. Neither moves. Thunder rolls. **END CLIP 1 on this held standoff.** (The next clip begins from exactly this state.)

## CAMERA RHYTHM

**descending master wide → low full-figure walk → slow push → hard detail on the draw → locked close-up on the shout → wide storm reveal → locked profile → locked low wide on the beast → held low two-shot**

- Keep the camera still whenever he shouts or the beast moves; let it travel only on the descent, the walk and the push.
- No shaky cam, no orbiting, no dutch angles.
- The close-ups (draw, shout, profile) happen while he is planted; the walk is always shown in full figure.
- Scale is the point of the last two shots: he must look small, the beast overwhelming.

## PERFORMANCE ARC

silent grief as he walks through the dead village → the breath gathers (chest rises, shoulders level) → the hand closes and the blade comes out → the roar, whole body behind it → stillness as the storm answers → eyes unmoving as the light dies → unmoved as the beast arrives → ready, blade raised.

## HARD CONTINUITY CONSTRAINTS

- same warrior identity, scar, beard and turban in every shot;
- one straight khanda in his right hand after the draw; one shield on his back, never a second shield;
- the same village geometry; the sun stays in the west until the storm covers it;
- no other people, no gore, no modern objects, no text, logos or watermarks;
- heavy, physical motion: boots that land with weight, cloth and sash with inertia, sand that jumps and settles.

It should feel like the moment a legend calls a god out, and the god answers.
