# Clip 2 — THE LAST CHARGE (24 seconds, vertical 9:16)

A 24-second vertical scene from a large-scale historical-mythic epic, photoreal, shot like a big-budget feature: one continuous sequence with motivated shot changes. It continues directly from the previous clip, where a lone warrior called a monster out of the storm and it came to him. Now he gathers himself, swears an oath and makes his last charge; the beast laughs and meets him; the film ends at the instant they collide. A long, quiet build, then an explosion. Every movement of the warrior lands like thunder; the beast's presence is overwhelming.

Image 1 is the warrior and the strict identity reference: his face, his heavy, powerful build, full black beard and thick upturned moustache, the pale scar through his left eyebrow, the small gold ring in his ear, the deep saffron safa turban, the knee-length riveted mail coat over a dark indigo quilted tunic, the four plain steel chest plates, the thick crimson sash, the plain steel forearm guards and the straight double-edged khanda. Image 3 is the beast and the village: the authority for the creature (long spiralling ridged horns, ember eyes, black hide cracked like a dried riverbed, the storm streaming off its back and mane) and for the set. Keep his exact likeness; never beautify or reshape his face; his face stays stable in every shot.

## MASTER GEOGRAPHY — NEVER CHANGE THIS

The same dead caravan village on the edge of the Thar desert.

- CENTRE (landmark 0): the square and the half-collapsed red-sandstone chhatri with a cracked dome, about eight metres tall.
- SOUTH: the lane of mud houses.
- EAST: the dry well and the overturned bullock cart.
- NORTH: the village edge and the dunes, where the beast stands.
- WEST: the sun, hidden behind the storm.

**The line of the charge:** from the chhatri straight NORTH across about forty metres of open, hard-packed sand to the beast. He always travels north; the beast always comes south. Seen from the east side he runs from screen left to screen right; that is only a framing aid, the world direction is what must hold.

**THE CAMERA MAY MOVE. THE SET ITSELF MUST NEVER ROTATE, MIRROR OR REARRANGE.**

## LIGHTING GEOGRAPHY

The storm covers the sky: the world is cold blue-grey, lit by lightning inside the clouds. At 0:05 a single shaft of low sun from the WEST breaks through a gap in the storm and falls on the warrior alone, warm gold, a column of light with sand glittering in it. From then on the warm light stays on him wherever he goes, and the beast stays in cold storm light with blue-white lightning on its horns. Warm against cold is the picture's argument; at the collision the two meet in one white-gold flare.

## 0:00–0:02.5 · THE DISTANCE

Wide from the east side of the square, both in profile, 28 mm, camera locked. The beast lifts its huge head away from him and draws back to the north edge of the village, rising to its full height inside the storm, horns against the lightning. Open sand now lies between them. He does not follow it with his feet, only with his eyes.

## 0:02.5–0:05 · ONE BREATH

Extreme close-up of his face in profile, 100 mm, camera locked. His eyes close. One long, controlled breath in through the nose, the chest and plates swelling, then out slowly through the beard. Grains of sand tremble on his cheek. The world's sound drops away to wind and his breathing.

## 0:05–0:07.5 · THE SUN BREAKS THROUGH (THE POWER RISES)

Low-angle medium, 50 mm, a slow push in. His eyes open. The shaft of low sun cuts through the storm and lands on him alone. Around his feet the sand lifts off the ground in a slow, widening ring, as if the air itself is rising around him; his sash and the tail of his turban lift and stream. The power shows in his body: his weight sinks into his legs, his shoulders square, his grip tightens until the knuckles whiten, his eyes lock on the beast.

## 0:07.5–0:13 · THE OATH

Close-up, low angle, 75 mm, camera locked. He brings the khanda upright in both hands in front of his face, the blade splitting the frame, the warm light running down the steel. He speaks slowly, deep and heavy, every word landing like a stone, his eyes never leaving the beast:

"I am Rudra Singh, son of Jaiveer Singh, sworn to this sand. This day you fall, and I fall with you."

A beat of silence after the last word.

## 0:13–0:15 · IT LAUGHS

Close-up on the beast's head in cold storm light, 50 mm. Its lips peel back from its teeth; a deep, rolling laugh shakes out of its chest like thunder rolling across the desert; lightning gathers and crackles between its horns; its ember eyes flare brighter. It lowers its horns toward him, bracing for impact, delighted.

## 0:15–0:16.5 · THE GROUND BREAKS

Extreme low close-up on his boot, 85 mm, camera at ground level. He drives his foot down; the hard-packed earth cracks in a star around the boot and caves slightly; sand and stones jump into the air. His knee bends and his weight loads forward. A sound like a thunderclap.

## 0:16.5–0:19.5 · HE LAUNCHES

Low wide tracking shot, the camera racing alongside him on his west side, 35 mm. He explodes north across the square: each stride detonates a burst of sand behind him with a crack like thunder, the khanda drawn back in both hands, the sash and turban tail streaming, the column of sunlight moving with him. His whole figure stays readable; the camera tracks with him and does not orbit.

## 0:19.5–0:21.5 · IT COMES TO MEET HIM

Very low wide from behind him, 24 mm. The beast drops its horns and charges south straight at him, still laughing, the storm tearing off its back in ribbons, the ground shaking under every step. He is small, running straight at something the size of a hill, and he does not slow down.

## 0:21.5–0:24 · THE CLASH (END)

Wide from the east side of the square, both in profile, 28 mm, camera locked. His blade meets the beast's horns in the centre of the frame. A ring of force blasts outward from the point of impact: sand, light and storm cloud are thrown back in a single expanding shockwave, the chhatri's cracked dome bursts apart, and the frame flares white-gold. **END CLIP 2 on the white-gold flash at the moment of impact.** Nothing after it.

## CAMERA RHYTHM

**locked wide geography → locked extreme close-up on the breath → slow push on the power rising → locked close-up on the oath → cold close-up on the laugh → ground-level detail on the boot → racing lateral track on the charge → low wide from behind into the beast → locked wide on the impact**

- The camera is still on every hard performance beat (breath, oath, laugh, impact) and moves only on the power-up push and the run.
- The charge is always shown in a wide that keeps his whole body readable; close-ups happen only while he is planted.
- No shaky cam, no orbits, no slow motion except the final flare.
- Cut between him and the beast like two forces answering each other: breath → sun → oath → laugh → ground → launch → charge → impact.

## PERFORMANCE ARC

stillness → one controlled breath → power rising through posture (weight sinks, shoulders square, grip tightens, eyes lock) → the oath, spoken like a vow → the ground breaks under him → full commitment → impact.

The beast: patient → amused → delighted → charging.

## HARD CONTINUITY CONSTRAINTS

- same warrior identity, scar, beard and turban in every shot;
- one straight khanda; one shield on his back, never a second shield;
- he runs north, the beast comes south; the chhatri stays in the square until the impact destroys its dome;
- warm sun on him only, cold storm light on the beast;
- no other people, no gore, no text, logos or watermarks;
- heavy, physical motion: footfalls with weight, sash and turban tail with inertia, sand that bursts and falls back.

It should feel like one man's whole life spent in a single charge.
