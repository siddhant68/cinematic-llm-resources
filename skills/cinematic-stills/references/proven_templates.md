# Our proven ChatGPT templates (as sent)

These are the exact messages we sent to ChatGPT image (GPT Image 2, **Chat mode**) on reels 007 to 010, HOLD STILL, the wind film and Violet. Each one produced a kept image. They are copied from the messages we sent (a few words lightly edited for this public copy). Reuse the structure and swap in your own film's facts.

**Pattern shared by all of them:**
1. say which attachment is what;
2. state 'photoreal vertical 9:16 film still' plus the framing;
3. give the staging with screen directions;
4. write the expression as muscles;
5. name the light source, side and colour;
6. give the camera height, distance, lens feel and aperture;
7. set the grade and grain;
8. say what must NOT be in frame;
9. close with the avoid line;
10. give the aspect ratio on its own line.

## T1 · Breakdown of any reference: look, light, lens, grade, pose
**When:** Stage 3. Paste the pin as an image, then this. It is aimed at any image model, not one tool's syntax. Use it for scene, light and mood pins, and for character trait pins.

```text
Look at this photo the way a fashion photographer and cinematographer would, and break it down in exact detail: the pose, head tilt, facial expression, body position, type of lighting, where the light comes from, the shadows, the lens and focal length, the depth of field, the colour grade, the textures, the outfit, the framing and the overall mood. Only describe what is actually visible, with correct anatomy. Then write one complete, technically precise image prompt that recreates this exact look and pose. Reply in two parts: 1. Visual breakdown 2. Image prompt, as one paragraph with no bullet points.
```

## T2 · Breakdown of a ROOM or a COSTUME only (never the face)
**When:** Stage 3. Use it for set and wardrobe pins, and for any film or show still (they contain actors: this prompt forbids describing the face). Fill in [ROOM / COSTUME].

```text
Look at this image the way a production designer, costume designer and cinematographer would. Break down ONLY the [ROOM / COSTUME] in exact detail. Do not describe any person's face, identity or likeness at all.

- For a ROOM: the architecture and materials, what stands where (left, centre, right, near, far), the windows and openings, the fabrics, the props, the practical light sources, where the light comes from, its colour and hardness, the shadows, the time of day, the colour palette, the textures and the lens feel.
- For a COSTUME: the garment's cut, fabric and weight, drape and folds, the neckline, sleeves and length, the belt and fastenings, trims and embroidery, colours, how it moves, and the hairstyle's structure (length, curls, braids and how they are pinned). No face.

Only describe what is actually visible. Then write one precise paragraph that recreates this [room / costume] on a different person or as an empty set.

Reply in two parts: 1. Breakdown 2. Recreation paragraph, as one paragraph with no bullet points.
```

## T3 · Breakdown of an EXPRESSION, muscle by muscle (transferable to our face)
**When:** Stage 3. For expression pins, one per emotional beat. The transferable paragraph goes into the Stage 4 rebuild.

```text
Look at this photo the way an acting coach and a portrait photographer would. Describe the facial expression muscle by muscle, only as it is visible, and do not describe who the person is.

Cover:
- the eyes: lids, gaze direction, focus or unfocus, wetness or shine;
- the brows: inner and outer ends;
- the forehead;
- the cheeks and any colour or flush;
- the nose;
- the mouth: the corners, lips parted or pressed, any tension;
- the jaw;
- the head tilt;
- the breath, neck, shoulders and hands.

Then say, in one line, what inner state this reads as, and whether it looks performed or real.

Finally write one paragraph that describes the same expression as physical muscle actions, so it can be recreated on a DIFFERENT person's face without copying this person.

Reply in three parts: 1. Muscle breakdown 2. What it reads as 3. Transferable expression paragraph.
```

## T6 · Targeted fix: keep everything, change only two things (reel 009)
**When:** Any stage. When a still is nearly right, attach it and name exactly what changes. Cheaper and safer than regenerating from scratch.

```text

Change only two things.

1. Her hair. Keep the copper-auburn colour and the length just past the jaw, but make it silky, smooth and full of volume: big, soft, glossy loose curls with a healthy natural shine where the sun catches them, thick and bouncy at the roots, the curls well defined and not frizzy, no flyaway fuzz. The wind lifts it gently so the whole shape moves as one soft mass, with two or three silky strands falling across her forehead.

2. The place behind her. Replace the hills, the cypress trees and the villa with an English-style flower farm: gently rolling green farmland divided by low hedgerows, a few round, leafy trees, and far off one small white farmhouse with a grey slate roof. A pale gravel farm lane with a strip of grass down the middle runs past her, and its verges are thick with wildflowers: red poppies, white ox-eye daisies, blue cornflowers, a few purple lupins and yellow buttercups. Above, a clear blue sky with big, bright white cumulus clouds. No cypress trees, no terracotta, no Mediterranean buildings.

Also add a thin, old, pale silvery scar that breaks the outer end of her left eyebrow (long healed, never red), faint but visible. Keep the same noon light from behind her left shoulder, the same natural film grade and fine grain, and her real skin texture. Avoid changing her face, retouching her skin, a different dress, text or watermarks.

Aspect ratio: 9:16 (vertical).
```

## T14 · Timed board: six panels, one escalating scene (reel 010)
**When:** Stage 6. For 2×3 boards. It fixes the light direction and 'what must not appear before panel N'.

```text
Using the three attached images, draw the storyboard of one continuous vertical film scene that runs 15 seconds, laid out as six panels, 2 across and 3 down, with timings. The first image is the woman: the reference for her face (thick low brows with a small scar in the left one, grey-green eyes, strong straight nose, full lips, the small mole low on her left cheek), her long dark wavy hair, cream check shirt, rust tank top and worn jeans. The second image is the place at dusk: the lonely fuel stop with its flat lit canopy and two pumps, the curving two-lane desert road, the flat horizon and the power lines. The third image is the dust storm and her pale green station wagon: the reference for the storm's look and for the car.

It should read like a real film pre-production board: six vertical 9:16 panels in a clean grid with thin black borders. On each panel, in small clean type: the panel number, its timecode, a short shot title in capitals and one short line of action. The pictures come first; the text is small but legible.

The scene is built on one idea: the audience should feel each emotion before they understand why. Calm, then connection, then unease, then fear, then shock, then escape. Her action never resets between panels; only the camera and the framing change, getting closer as the emotion rises and then exploding outward.

1 · 0:00–0:02 · ESTABLISH · Very wide, still camera. The fuel stop tiny under a huge clean dusk sky, her car under the canopy, her a small figure leaning on it. The horizon is completely clear.
2 · 0:02–0:05 · CONNECT · Medium shot, slow push-in. She leans on the open door, eyes closed, face to the last warm light, a small private smile.
3 · 0:05–0:08 · DISRUPT · Medium-wide, the camera drifts. The gold light turns brown, a hanging sign on the canopy starts to swing, dust snakes across the road, birds lift off the power line. She has not noticed yet. Still no storm visible.
4 · 0:08–0:10 · REACT · Tight close-up. Her smile is gone, hair whipping across her face, eyes locked on something past the camera, a held breath. We still do not see it.
5 · 0:10–0:12 · REVEAL · Wide reverse angle from behind her: a kilometre-high wall of dust swallowing the horizon and rolling toward the fuel stop.
6 · 0:12–0:15 · ESCAPE · Low fast tracking shot in front of the car: the wagon tears down the road, headlights on, the dust wall filling everything behind it.

Hold these fixed in all six panels: her face and outfit, the pale green wagon, the fuel stop and road layout, and one light direction (the sun just below the horizon ahead of her, then hidden by the storm). The storm must not appear anywhere in panels 1 to 4. No extra people, no text other than the panel labels, no logos, number plates or watermarks. Photoreal film-still style in every panel, natural analogue colour: dusk blue and peach at the start, rust and ochre at the end.

Aspect ratio: 9:16 (vertical).
```
