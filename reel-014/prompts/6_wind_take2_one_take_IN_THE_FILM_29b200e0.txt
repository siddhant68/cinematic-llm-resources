# HOLD STILL, part 2 — "The Wind Takes the Flowers" (one take)

ONE continuous 20-second vertical 9:16 shot with NO cuts: a single camera that moves like a slow dolly from the first frame to the last. Photoreal live-action cinema. A bright, gusty noon on an English flower farm. She has just forgiven him for being late; he is handing her the wildflowers he picked. The wind takes them, and the loss turns into a laugh. Unhurried: every moment has room to breathe.

## REFERENCES

Image 5 = her face and flying hair when the gust hits (about 5–6 s): expression only, not a separate shot.

Image 7 = the poppy action (15–20 s): four moments inside this same continuous shot; it adds no cuts. Its labels are notes for you only and never appear in the video.

## THE PLACE — NEVER CHANGES

She sits on the bonnet of the faded red car, frame left. He stands facing her, frame right, an arm's length away, both seen side-on. Behind them the wildflower field runs back to dark hedgerows and round trees under a bright sky of fast white clouds. The high noon sun sits behind her from frame left and stays bright the whole time; the sky never darkens. The wind always blows from frame left to frame right.

## THE SCENE, MOMENT BY MOMENT (the air is described in every moment)

(0.0–4.5 s) CONNECT, THE WIND COMING. The camera pushes in very slowly toward her. Their fingertips meet on the stems of the small bouquet. Her eyes are on his, and a soft, closed smile arrives. THE AIR: a gentle breeze; her curls sway on her shoulders; the poppies around them nod. From 2.5 s, far behind them in this same frame, a band of wind races across the field from frame left: the grass and flowers flatten in a rolling wave coming toward them, and a hiss swells into a roar. They do not notice.

(4.5–8.0 s) THE GUST TAKES THE FLOWERS. The camera has reached a medium close-up of her face and shoulders, with his hand and the bouquet at the right edge of frame, and holds still. THE AIR: at 4.5 s the gust hits hard. Her hair lifts off her shoulders all at once and streams to frame right; her dress snaps against her; his jacket flaps. The bouquet is torn apart in their hands, never as one bunch: the stems scatter, and the flower heads rip off one by one and whip out of frame to the right, each one gone in about a second, tumbling and blurred. Loose petals from the field stream past the lens from left to right the whole time; nothing floats or hangs. HER FACE (Image 5): (4.8) her eyes widen; (5.1) her breath catches and her lips part; (5.5) the last stems slip from her fingers; (5.8–8.0) her eyes follow the flowers up and away to frame right, and her head tips back after her eyes.

(8.0–10.5 s) THE WIND DIES. The camera eases slowly back into a medium two-shot of both of them, side-on. THE AIR: at 8.5 s the wind stops. Her hair falls back onto her shoulders under its own weight in half a second, a few strands settling last. The last petals flutter straight down and land on the bonnet, her lap and the grass. The flattened field springs back upright. By 10 s the air is clear and still, just a soft breeze. They both stare up and to frame right at the empty sky, then at each other.

(10.5–15.0 s) HER LAUGH — let it run. The camera holds. He looks down at his empty hand, still held out: the inner ends of his brows lift, and the corners of his mouth drop under his moustache. (11.5) She looks from his hand to his face. (12.0) The corner of her mouth goes. (12.3) She bursts into a real laugh and leans right back on both hands on the bonnet, head tipping back, eyes creased shut, shoulders shaking (Image 6). The laugh keeps rolling for about two seconds, then (14.3) it softens into breathless giggles as she sits back up and looks at him again. THE AIR: soft breeze; her curls bounce and swing with every shake of her laugh; the grass sways behind them.

(15.0–20.0 s) THE POPPY — slow, clear, every movement readable (Image 7). The camera drifts in a little to a medium close two-shot and then stays completely still. THE AIR: a soft breeze lifts a few of her curls; the field sways gently behind them. (15.0) Her eyes find a single red poppy caught on his jacket collar. (15.5–16.3) She reaches over and picks it off by its short green stem between her thumb and finger. (16.3–17.0) She holds it up between their faces for a beat; he looks at it, then at her, pressing his lips together to keep a straight face. (17.0–18.2) She slowly slides the stem behind his ear, the ear facing the camera, right next to the silver hoop, her fingertips smoothing his curls over it. (18.2–18.6) Her hand comes away and rests flat on his chest; the red poppy is clearly visible behind his ear, nothing covering it. (18.6–20.0) He gives in and laughs helplessly through his nose, eyes creased, looking at her; she smiles at him warmly, her eyes on his. Still moving on the last frame.

## PERFORMANCE

Every change of face is caused by something we have just seen; eyes move first, then brows, breath, mouth, head. Real, unposed faces with visible pores. Neither of them looks into the lens, and neither speaks.

## SOUND

A gentle breeze; a hiss swelling to a roar through the grass by 4.5 s; the rustle of torn stems; the wind cutting out at 8.5 s; her long real laugh; his breath out through his nose. No music, no dialogue.

## AVOID

Any cut, any change of shot, any jump in framing; the camera following the flowers; the bouquet flying away in one piece; flowers or petals floating, hovering or frozen in the air; a dark or stormy sky; a wide open gaping mouth; hair covering her whole face for more than a moment; her hand covering the poppy at the end; slow motion; confetti, sparkles or glow; text or labels from the reference images; any change to their faces, hair or clothes; extra people; looking into the lens; lips moving as if talking; a kiss; a frozen final frame.
