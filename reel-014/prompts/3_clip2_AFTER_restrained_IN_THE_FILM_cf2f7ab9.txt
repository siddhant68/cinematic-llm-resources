# HOLD STILL — "Bad at Being Cross"

One continuous 16-second vertical 9:16 shot with no cuts. Photoreal live-action cinema. It is a cold, gusty noon on an English flower farm. A young woman has waited too long for him, sitting on the bonnet of a faded red car. He finally walks up the lane with one hand hidden behind his back. She decides to stay cross with him, and she is very bad at it. This is a quiet, real moment between two people, not a performance.

Image 1 is the opening frame: begin exactly on it. Image 2 is her identity: face, freckles, shoulder-length loose copper-auburn curls, the sunflower-yellow strapped sundress with white bands at the neckline and hem, and green slippers. Image 3 is her six-angle sheet. Image 4 is the car. Image 5 is him: dark messy curls, moustache, cracked brown leather jacket, white T-shirt, dark jeans, steel watch.

---

## MASTER GEOGRAPHY — NEVER CHANGE THIS

The faded cherry-red car is parked on a pale gravel farm lane, its front toward the camera. The lane runs from beside the car's right front wheel (frame right) straight back to low hedgerows, round trees and a small white farmhouse far off. Verges of red poppies, white daisies, blue cornflowers and purple lupins line both sides. SHE sits on the left half of the bonnet (frame left). HE walks toward the car along the lane, rounds the car's right front corner, and stops there, beside her, facing her.

A high noon sun sits behind her from frame left. It rims her curls, and soft cloud fill lights her face. The wind comes from frame left.

**THE CAMERA MOVES SLOWLY. SHE STAYS SEATED ON THE BONNET FOR THE WHOLE SHOT. THE CAR, LANE AND SUN NEVER MOVE, MIRROR OR REARRANGE.**

---

## WIND AND HAIR — GUSTS WITH LULLS

The wind comes in gusts, with softer lulls between them. Gusts arrive at about 1.5 s, 7 s and 12 s. In each gust, her loose lengths lift off her shoulders and stream toward frame right, the grass bends in a wave and a few petals cross the frame. In each lull the curls fall back under their own weight, sway, and settle, and loose strands drift near her cheek. Her hair is shoulder-length, loose, soft curls and waves with real weight: the lengths hang down on her shoulders and swing when she moves her head, then settle half a beat after her head stops. Her hair is never frozen in one shape. His curls move in the same gusts. Her skirt hem flutters in each gust.

---

## PERFORMANCE STYLE — RESTRAINED NATURALISM

She shows what she feels with her body: posture, arms, where she looks, her breath. Her face stays calm and natural. There is only one change in her face in the whole shot: the small smile she cannot stop at the end, at about a third of its full size. Her eyes move first, her head follows, then her body. Between changes she is simply still, breathing, with her hair moving.

---

## OPENING CAMERA (0–6 s)

The camera is low, at her knee height, about three metres in front of the car, with a 50 mm lens feel. It holds her full figure on the bonnet, the green slippers visible, and the lane receding behind her on frame right. The camera holds still here.

## WAITING (0–4.5 s)

Her slippers swing slowly, alternating, and tap the bumper. The fingers of her right hand drum on the bonnet. Her eyes are on the sky, bored. The first gust blows a curl across her face. She blows it straight up off her forehead with one puff of breath, it flops back down, and she leaves it there.

## SHE SEES HIM (4.5–6 s)

Far down the lane on frame right, he is walking toward the car with his right hand hidden behind his back. Her eyes find him first. The drumming stops. One slipper stops mid-swing. Then her head turns after her eyes, and her curls swing and settle.

## SHE DECIDES TO BE CROSS (6–9.5 s)

The camera begins a very slow push-in and rises gently toward her eye level. It keeps pushing for the rest of the shot.

She sits up straighter and smooths her skirt down over her knees with both palms. She crosses her arms. She turns her head away to frame left and looks out over the field. She holds that pose while her face stays calm and still. The 7-second gust lifts her curls; they swing back as it passes. Behind her, he walks closer down the lane.

## HE ARRIVES (9.5–11 s)

He rounds the car's right front corner and stops there, frame right, about an arm's length from her, facing her in three-quarter profile. He brings his right hand from behind his back, holding a small, loose fistful of wildflowers from the verge (red poppies, white daisies, blue cornflowers). He holds them low between them, near her knee. She does not look yet. A held beat.

## SHE GIVES IN (11–16 s)

Her eyes slide to frame right, down to the flowers, and her head follows. Her eyes rise from the flowers to his face: up and to frame right, straight into his eyes. She breathes out once through her nose. Her shoulders drop. Her arms loosen and uncross. The corner of her mouth lifts into a small, closed-lip smile that she is trying to hold back. She glances down at the flowers once, then her eyes return to his and stay there.

The 12-second gust blows a curl across her cheek; she lets it stay. He watches her face, completely still, and a slight half-smile appears under his moustache.

End on a medium close-up of her face, looking at him, with his shoulder and the flowers soft at the right edge of the frame and her curls still moving in the wind.

---

## CAMERA RHYTHM

**low full-figure hold → very slow push-in and rise → medium two-shot across the car's front corner → medium close-up on her face**

- There is one continuous take with no cut and no second setup.
- While he hands over the flowers, the camera keeps its slow push steady and adds no other movement.
- Keep her eyes the sharpest point of the frame from 11 s to the end.

---

## EYELINES

- 4.5–6 s: she looks far down the lane, frame right.
- 6–11 s: she looks away, frame left, at the field.
- 11–16 s: she looks at his eyes, up and to frame right, and at the flowers once.
- He looks only at her. Neither of them ever looks into the lens.

---

## SOUND

Wind rising and falling in gusts, grass hiss, her slippers tapping the bumper, his footsteps on gravel getting closer, and her one breath out through her nose. No music. Nobody speaks.

---

## HARD CONSTRAINTS

- Same faces as Images 2 and 5; same shoulder-length loose curls, freckles, dress, slippers, car.
- She stays seated on the bonnet; she does not take the flowers yet.
- One continuous shot; no cut.
- Her face stays natural. No pouting or pushed-out lips (apart from the one quick puff of breath at the curl), no squinting, no raised eyebrow, no eye-roll, no nose scrunch, no wide grin, no laugh, no lips moving as if talking.
- No extra people, no slow motion, no music, no text, logos or number plates.

The feeling is a person trying very hard to stay annoyed and losing, quietly, to a handful of wildflowers.
