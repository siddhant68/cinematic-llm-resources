# HOLD STILL — Clip B: "The Moment He Falls"

A 13-second, vertical 9:16, photoreal live-action sequence in ONE continuous, unbroken shot, with no cuts at all. A cold, windy noon on an English flower farm. A young woman with full, silky copper-auburn curls, dense freckles and a sunflower-yellow 1950s strapped cotton sundress (white band along the neckline) stands on a gravel farm lane in front of a faded red car, facing the man who has just offered her wildflowers. She has been pretending to be cross with him. Here the wind undoes her, and she lets him see her. The camera begins as his point of view on her face and, without any cut, drifts slowly around to discover his face beside hers. The emotion is quiet, private and real: a person caught in a true moment, never a performance.

Image 1 is the opening frame — begin exactly on it. Image 2 is her identity (face, curls, freckles, marks, dress). Image 3 is her six-angle sheet. Image 4 is her face and light at about 9 s (for expression and light only). Image 5 is his identity. Image 6 is his face and expression at the end (for identity and expression only, not framing).

---

## MASTER GEOGRAPHY — NEVER CHANGE THIS

A straight pale-gravel farm lane with a grass strip down its middle runs through a wide field of pale-green grass and verges of red poppies, white ox-eye daisies, blue cornflowers and purple lupins. The faded cherry-red car is parked on the lane. SHE stands on the lane about one metre in front of the car's bonnet. HE stands on the lane facing her, about a metre and a half away, his back to the open field and the far hedgerows. Beyond her: the car, then low hedgerows, round leafy trees and a small white farmhouse far off. Beyond him: open field, hedgerows, big white clouds.

The high noon sun sits behind HER left shoulder, over the car's side of the lane. Seen from his side it rims her curls and leaves her face in soft cloud fill until it breaks out. As the camera arcs round behind her shoulder, that same high sun is now in front of him and above, warm on his face, while it rims her curls from behind; it never moves.

**THE CAMERA MOVES SLOWLY AND CONTINUOUSLY. NEITHER OF THEM TRAVELS. THE SET, THE CAR AND THE SUN NEVER ROTATE, MIRROR OR REARRANGE.**

---

## CONTINUITY LEDGER

- Wildflowers: the same small, loose fistful (red poppies, white daisies, blue cornflowers) stays in HIS right hand, lowered at his side, below the frame; at most a soft blur of red at the bottom edge near the end. She has NOT taken them yet.
- His leather jacket: still on him, open, over the white T-shirt.
- Her hair: fully loose, wind-blown; after the tuck, one side sits behind her right ear while the loose strands keep blowing.
- Her hands: empty.

---

## WIND AND HAIR — CONTINUOUS, THE WHOLE CLIP

A steady, gusting wind blows across the lane from frame left for all 13 seconds. Her curls are NEVER still: they lift, stream sideways, tangle and resettle in constant, weighted motion, with loose strands flicking across her cheek, forehead and lips again and again, even after she tucks them. Hair has real weight: it swings, falls back and settles, then lifts again with the next gust. When his face comes into frame, his dark curls move in the same wind. Grass, petals and the edge of her dress strap move with it.

---

## PERFORMANCE STYLE — RESTRAINED NATURALISM

Every expression is small — about a third of its full size — and starts in her eyes before it reaches her mouth. She is not posing and not playing to anyone but him. No mugging, no pouting or kiss-shaped lips, no squinting or squeezed-shut eyes, no big eyebrow lifts, no head tilts for effect. Between changes she is simply still, breathing, her hair moving. Changes are slow and soft; the stillness between them matters as much as the changes.

---

## OPENING STATE, CAMERA AND LIGHT (0.0 s)

His point of view, the camera at her eye level about a metre and a half from her, 85 mm intimate lens feel, shallow focus on her eyes, the car and field soft behind her. Medium close-up, waist to just above her head. A cloud covers the sun: her face is in soft, even, bright light; her curls carry a faint rim. A gust has just blown her curls across her face, one eye showing through, one hand just lifting toward her chin — exactly as in Image 1.

Camera, one unbroken take: a very slow push-in from this medium close-up to a close-up of her face (0–8.5 s); then, without stopping, a slow arc around the two of them, about a half-turn to her left, ending just over her left shoulder looking at him (9–13 s). No cut anywhere, no shake.

---

## THE WIND WINS (0.0–3.8 s)

She stays still for a moment behind the blowing strands, her visible eye on him. The corner of her mouth gives way first: a small, quiet laugh escapes through her nose, just a breath, her eyes softening. She lifts two fingers and draws the curls back off her face in one unhurried sweep, tucking them behind her right ear; the next gust immediately blows a few strands back across her cheek, and she lets them be.

---

## SHE LETS HIM SEE HER (3.8–6.0 s)

Her hand lowers. Her eyes come up and settle on the lens — on him — and stay. A calm, open look. One slow blink. At about 5 s the cloud slides off the sun: warm light spills across her face, her freckles and the fine hair along her cheek catch it, and her eyes brighten from the cold wind.

---

## THE SMILE (6.0–9.0 s)

A flicker of shyness: her eyes drop for a second and her lower lip is caught lightly in her teeth. Then her eyes return to the lens and a smile arrives slowly — lips closed at first, then just parting, the left corner a little higher than the right — soft, warm, unguarded, as in Image 4. Her curls keep blowing across her cheek. Hold on her face.

## THE CAMERA DISCOVERS HIM (9.0–13.0 s)

Without any cut, the camera keeps moving: it arcs slowly and smoothly around to her left, as if stepping out of his eyes, about a half-turn around the pair, until it rests just over her left shoulder — her blowing curls and the edge of her cheek soft in the foreground of the frame, and HIS face beyond her, three-quarter to camera, sharp. Focus pulls gently from her to him during the drift. The sun, the car and the lane stay exactly where they were; only the camera has moved.

He is looking at her, not at the camera. He is completely still, eyes soft and fixed on her face, lips closed, his curls moving in the wind — as in Image 6. Then, slowly, a small, helpless half-smile pulls up one corner of his mouth under the moustache, and he lets out a breath through his nose. He is gone. Hold to the last frame, both their hair still moving.

---

## CAMERA RHYTHM

**slow POV push from medium close-up to close-up on her face → the same take arcs a half-turn around them to over her shoulder → focus pulls to his face**

- The push is slow enough that the viewer barely notices it.
- Keep her eyes the sharpest point of the frame until the drift begins.
- No cut of any kind, no cutaway to the flowers, no wide shot.
- The drift is slow and smooth, like a dolly move; the camera never jumps.

---

## PERFORMANCE ARC

Her: wind-flustered → a laugh she can't hold → hair tucked, guard down → steady eyes on him → sunlight → a moment of shyness → a slow, real smile.
Him: stillness → a half-smile he can't stop → a breath out. Neither of them speaks.

---

## SOUND

Wind across the field and in her hair, grass hiss, the smallest breath of a laugh through her nose, his slow breath out. No music, no dialogue.

---

## HARD CONTINUITY CONSTRAINTS

- Same faces and identities as Images 2 and 5; her freckles, curls and yellow strapped sundress with its white neckline band unchanged; his jacket, moustache, lip scar and silver hoop unchanged; no dirt on his face.
- Same lane, car position and sun position throughout the camera move.
- Her hair is always moving; never frozen.
- She looks into the lens only from 3.8 s to 9.0 s; he never looks into the lens.
- No cuts, no jump in framing; one continuous take.
- No overacting, no pouting, no squinting, no wide grin, no open-mouthed laugh, no lips moving as if talking, no tears, no nodding.
- No extra people, no slow motion, no music, no text, logos or number plates.

The film should feel like the few seconds you would remember for the rest of your life: wind, sun, and a face letting its guard down.
