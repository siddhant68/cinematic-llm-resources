# HOLD STILL, part 2 — "The Wind Takes the Flowers"

One continuous 20-second vertical 9:16 sequence in six shots. Photoreal live-action cinema. A cold, gusty noon on an English flower farm. She has just forgiven him for being late; he is finally handing her the wildflowers he picked. Then the wind takes them. The feeling climbs one step per shot: tender, something coming, surprise, wonder, loss that turns funny, together. Each shot is caused by the one before it. The action never resets between shots; only the framing changes.

## MASTER GEOGRAPHY — NEVER CHANGE THIS

The faded red car is parked on a pale gravel farm lane in the middle of a wide field of red poppies, white daisies, blue cornflowers and purple lupins. SHE sits on the bonnet, on the left side of the car. HE stands just off the car's front corner, facing her, an arm's length away. Behind them: low dark hedgerows, round trees, a small white farmhouse far off, and a huge sky of fast white clouds. A high noon sun sits behind her from frame left. The wind always comes from frame left and blows toward frame right. The car, lane, sun and field never move, mirror or rearrange.

## THE GUST — PLAN IT LIKE WEATHER

- 0–3.5 s, lull: soft air; her curls sway on her shoulders. A distant hiss begins.
- 3.5–6.5 s, approach: a visible band of wind travels across the field from frame left toward them. Poppies, grass and lupins bow flat in a rolling wave, one after another; a cloud shadow races ahead of it; a line of petals peels off the verge. The hiss grows into a roar.
- 6.5–7.3 s, impact: all her hair lifts off her shoulders at once and streams to frame right; her skirt presses against her legs on one side and billows on the other; his curls flatten and his jacket flaps open; the bouquet's stems bend, then tear out of both their hands.
- 7.3–12.5 s, peak: the bouquet bursts apart in the air. Whole flowers and loose petals spiral up and away over the field toward frame right, at three depths, glowing against the clouds.
- 12.5–14 s, release: the wind drops. Her hair falls back onto her shoulders under its own weight in about half a second, a few strands settling last; petals drift down between them.
- 14–20 s: soft breeze, a few petals still falling, her curls lifting gently.

Hair is always weighted and always rim-lit by the sun behind her, framed against the dark hedgerows so every strand reads. When she moves her head, the hair follows a moment later and the ends settle last. Real wind physics: petals and flowers, never confetti or sparkles.

## PERFORMANCE STYLE

Every change of face is caused by something the camera has just shown. Eyes move first, then brows, then breath, then mouth, then head. Faces are real and unposed, pores visible. Neither of them ever looks into the lens, and neither speaks.

## SHOT STRUCTURE

(0.0–3.5 s) CONNECT — tender. Medium two-shot from the side at her eye level, a slow push-in. He holds the small bouquet out between them. She reaches for it; their fingertips meet on the stems. Her eyes are on his, and a soft, closed smile arrives. Her curls sway in the lull.

(3.5–6.5 s) DISRUPT — something is coming. Wide and low from the field on frame left, looking toward the car. (Image 6, G1 then G2.) Only the world changes: the gust travels toward them through the flowers as a rolling wave, the cloud shadow racing ahead, petals peeling off the verge. The two of them are small by the red car, still looking at each other, unaware. The roar builds.

(6.5–9.0 s) REACT — surprise. Close-up on her face, 85 mm feel, the camera still. (6.5) The gust hits: her whole mass of curls lifts and streams to frame right, strands across her cheek. (6.8) Her eyes widen. (7.0) Her breath catches and her lips part. (7.3) The stems rip out of her fingers, out of frame. (7.6–9.0) Her eyes follow them up and to frame right, then her head tips back after her eyes. We do NOT see the flowers in the sky yet.

(9.0–12.5 s) REVEAL — wonder. Low angle looking up past their two raised hands (Image 6, G3): the bouquet is breaking apart a metre above them, stems separating and spinning. Then the camera tilts slowly up with the flowers as they burst apart: poppies, daisies and cornflowers spiral up and away toward frame right against the huge clouds, some close to the lens and blurred, some tiny and high. The two of them are small at the bottom of frame, heads tilted back: her hair streaming, his jacket flapping.

(12.5–16.0 s) TURN — loss, then it's funny. Medium two-shot from the side again (Image 6, G4). The wind drops and her hair falls back onto her shoulders. He looks down at his empty hand, still held out: the inner ends of his brows lift, and the corners of his mouth drop under his moustache. (13.3) She brings her eyes down from the sky to his face. (13.8) The corner of her mouth goes. (14.2) She laughs: a real laugh, eyes creasing, head tipping back, one hand coming up to her chest, her curls bouncing with it.

(16.0–20.0 s) PAYOFF — together. One continuous medium close two-shot from the side, the camera still (Image 7). (16.0) Her laugh fades into a smile, eyes still on his face; one red poppy is caught in her curls above her left ear, and her fingers find it. (17.0) She holds the single poppy up between them by its short stem; he looks at it, then at her, pressing his lips together under his moustache to keep a straight face. (18.0) She leans in and tucks it behind his ear, next to the silver hoop, her fingertips in his curls; the corner of his mouth gives way. (19.0–20.0) He laughs helplessly through his nose, eyes creased, still looking at her; she smiles at him warmly, her hand resting on his jacket collar, her eyes on his. Petals drift down between them and her curls lift in a soft breeze. The shot is still moving on the last frame.

## SOUND

A soft breeze; a distant hiss swelling into a roar of wind through grass at 6.5 s; paper-dry rustle of stems; the wind falling away at 12.5 s; her real laugh; his breath out through his nose. No music, no dialogue.

## AVOID

Any text, letters, numbers or labels copied from the storyboards; more than six shots or any extra cut; a kiss; any change to their faces, hair, clothes or the car; extra people or vehicles; either of them looking into the lens; lips moving as if talking; a scream; a posed grin; tears; confetti, sparkles or glow effects; slow motion; the flowers visible in the sky before 9 s; a frozen final frame.
